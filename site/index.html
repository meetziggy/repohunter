What a verdict is made of

Every verdict comes with its reasons, so you or your agent can disagree with it.

Fit for your project

Scored against what you're building (from your config), plus popularity, freshness, health and maturity. A heuristic over public GitHub data. Guidance, not a guarantee.

Maintenance

Archived repos are a SKIP. Last push, releases and contributors feed the score. Activity isn't quality; it's a reason to look closer.

License

Permissive, weak or strong copyleft, source-available, so a GPL or BUSL dependency doesn't end up inside something you sell. Facts and a call, never legal advice.

Safety scan

Reads the repo's text and agent config files (CLAUDE.md, skills, MCP configs) for prompt-injection phrasing, hidden zero-width or bidi text, curl | bash installs, base64-decoded shell and leaked secrets. Heuristic checks, not a certification: clean means these checks found nothing.

Hardware fit

Reads your machine's RAM and flags GPU-only or training-scale projects before you clone them.

Integration plan

For a repo you'll adopt: what you gain, build versus reuse, and what integrating it costs. Uses an LLM if you configure one (Ollama or your own key). Scoring and scanning need none.

Try any repo, no install

The quick read, from public GitHub data, scored in your browser. It doesn't know your project, so its verdict can be higher than the agent tool's, which also scores fit. The license read and safety scan run in the CLI and the agent tool.

Why use RepoHunter instead of ChatGPT or Claude Code?

Use your assistant to reason and build; use RepoHunter for an adoption decision someone else can review. The full answer, including what isn't proven yet:

Use a general AI assistant to reason, explain, and help you build. Use RepoHunter when you need an open-source adoption decision tied to inspectable repository evidence, a consistent method, and a result someone else can review. They work well together: RepoHunter gathers and structures the evidence; you and your assistant decide what that evidence means for your project.

RepoHunter is read-only, shows the signals behind its guidance, and runs locally so you stay in control of your workflow. Missing or stale evidence should remain visible rather than being filled in with a confident guess. Its GO / MAYBE / SKIP result is a heuristic for a use case—not a security audit, a guarantee, or a verdict on a maintainer.

We do not yet claim RepoHunter is cheaper or faster. There is no controlled RepoHunter-versus-ChatGPT-or-Claude-Code benchmark yet. A defensible comparison must publish dated runs with wall-clock time, provider-reported tokens, tool calls, current prices, evidence completeness, and repeated-run consistency. Until then, the measured benefit is still an open question; the immediate value is a more reproducible, reviewable decision process.
