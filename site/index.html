What RepoHunter does

It sits between your agent's plan and the install. One question, asked every time: does a good version of this already exist, and is it safe to bring in?

Your agent is about to…

  • write a feature from scratch"add a Markdown renderer"
  • install a plugin, skill or MCP server"set up a token saver"
  • add a dependency you'll ship"use this for invoices"
RepoHunter checks
  • Does it already exist?live GitHub search, ranked
  • Is it maintained, or archived?last push, releases, contributors
  • Can you ship its license?copyleft, source-available, none
  • Anything sketchy in it?pattern-based scan of its text and agent files (CLI)
  • Will it run on your machine?reads your RAM; flags GPU-only projects

You get

  • GO reuse itmaintained, licensed, fits
  • MAYBE check firstwith the reason to check
  • SKIP build or pick anotherarchived, unlicensed or weak

Why use RepoHunter instead of ChatGPT or Claude Code?

Use your assistant to reason and build; use RepoHunter for an adoption decision someone else can review. The full answer, including what isn't proven yet:

Use a general AI assistant to reason, explain, and help you build. Use RepoHunter when you need an open-source adoption decision tied to inspectable repository evidence, a consistent method, and a result someone else can review. They work well together: RepoHunter gathers and structures the evidence; you and your assistant decide what that evidence means for your project.

RepoHunter is read-only, shows the signals behind its guidance, and runs locally so you stay in control of your workflow. Missing or stale evidence should remain visible rather than being filled in with a confident guess. Its GO / MAYBE / SKIP result is a heuristic for a use case—not a security audit, a guarantee, or a verdict on a maintainer.

We do not yet claim RepoHunter is cheaper or faster. There is no controlled RepoHunter-versus-ChatGPT-or-Claude-Code benchmark yet. A defensible comparison must publish dated runs with wall-clock time, provider-reported tokens, tool calls, current prices, evidence completeness, and repeated-run consistency. Until then, the measured benefit is still an open question; the immediate value is a more reproducible, reviewable decision process.
