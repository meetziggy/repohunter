# Why RepoHunter exists

Every single week, thousands of people around the world sit down and give away something they built —
for free, under a license that lets you use it, change it, and ship it. No paywall. No ask. Just
*"here, this might help you."*

It's one of the most quietly generous things humans do. And most of it is never found.

## The two tragedies

Discovery is broken. There are millions of repositories now, and the systems that surface them reward
what's *already* popular — the rich get richer, the algorithm feeds the algorithm. So two things happen
at the same time, and both are a waste:

1. **Brilliant work dies in obscurity.** The project with twelve stars that solves your exact problem —
   better than the one with forty thousand — you'll never see it. Its author will eventually stop,
   thinking nobody cared. People *did* care. They just never found it.
2. **We reinvent what already exists.** Not out of ego — out of blindness. You can't reuse what you
   can't find, so you rebuild it, worse, and burn a week you'll never get back.

## The part nobody says out loud

The entire digital world runs on the unpaid, unseen labor of maintainers — the
[one person in Nebraska](https://xkcd.com/2347/) holding up a stack the whole internet leans on. They
don't do it for money. Most of them don't even get a thank-you. Open source is a gift economy, and the
givers are mostly invisible.

And now AI writes more and more of the code. Point an agent at a problem and it reaches for the same ten
popular libraries every time — because popularity is the only signal it has. If we're not careful, the
age of AI turns a vibrant, diverse commons into a monoculture, and the long tail of great, weird,
perfect-for-you software goes dark for good.

There's a third waste, and nobody itemizes it: every token an AI generates burns real energy —
electricity and water in a data center somewhere. When millions of us regenerate the same auth flow,
the same RAG pipeline, the same library that *already exists*, that's redundant compute for zero new
value. The greenest code is the code you never regenerate. **Reuse, don't re-burn.**

That's the world RepoHunter was built to push back on.

## What we're actually here to do

RepoHunter is a love letter to open source, disguised as a tool. Its job is simple and it never
changes:

- **Find the *right* code — for you.** Not the most-starred. The best fit for *your* project, *your*
  stack, *your* machine — underdogs included.
- **Tell you the truth about it.** Honestly: does it fit, will it run on your hardware, what does
  adopting it cost, is it even worth it, or should you build your own? No hype. No paid placement. The
  honesty *is* the product.
- **Send the credit home.** Every path leads back to the author. If RepoHunter helps you find something
  worth using, it puts a **★ Star it** button in front of you and nudges you to thank the human who
  gave it away. Reuse, *with gratitude.*

Reusing great work isn't stealing — it's the whole point of open source. The theft is letting it go
unseen and unthanked.

## Who's behind it

Built by a **veteran and his AI, in the open** — a guy who fell in love with open source by watching it,
and the AI that helps him build. It's free forever (MIT), built *on* open source and given back, and in
place of donations to us, it points support at those who served on four legs and two.

We don't know if it goes viral. We know it's the right thing to build.

**Great code deserves to be found. The people who give it away deserve to be thanked.
Let's keep the commons alive.**
